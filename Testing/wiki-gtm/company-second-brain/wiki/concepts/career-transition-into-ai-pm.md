# Career Transition into AI PM

## Summary
The dominant job to be done for our students is not learning AI — it is
converting existing seniority into AI credibility. Three of eleven reviewers
were actively between roles, and the reviews that describe outcomes describe
*confidence* rather than skills. Meanwhile the market's demand signal is
explicit and unmet, and we have no data on whether anybody actually got hired.

## Key Facts

**Students explicitly in transition (3 of 11):**
- [[microsoft]] — Kartiki Saraf, ex Microsoft, expects the specialization to
  accelerate her re-entry into the workforce (`MAVEN_FEEDBACK` row 9,
  `private_note`, paraphrased).
- [[bank-ozk]] — Avinendra Chauhan, in a transitional phase and job searching,
  while exploring building something of his own (`MAVEN_FEEDBACK` row 8,
  `private_note`, paraphrased).
- [[unaffiliated-students]] — Shankar Ranganathan, company field reads
  "Currently between jobs" (`MAVEN_FEEDBACK` row 7).

**Outcome language centres on confidence, not capability:**
- [[t-mobile]] — Sunil Gangavarapu: the course gave him confidence that his
  product, data, API, and enterprise-systems background translates into the AI
  world (`MAVEN_FEEDBACK` row 10, paraphrased). He then said publicly that the
  program bridges the PM-to-AI gap better than anything else he tried
  (`LINKEDIN_POSTS` row 2, 187 reactions).
- [[loblaw-companies]] — Venkat krishnan Chellappa: arrived with PM experience,
  and can now confidently claim he can contribute as a PM from an AI perspective
  (`MAVEN_FEEDBACK` row 3, `private_note`, paraphrased).
- [[microsoft]] — Kartiki Saraf: increased confidence in building and managing
  Agentic AI programs (`MAVEN_FEEDBACK` row 9, `private_note`, paraphrased).

**The demand side, from an actual buyer of this talent:**
- A Head of Product at a fintech startup posted asking where people properly
  learn RAG, evals, and agent architecture, wanting AI PMs who understand them
  rather than buzzwords (`LINKEDIN_POSTS` row 5, posted 2026-06-22).
- The post drew only 62 reactions but **33 comments — the highest comment count
  in the table.** Low approval, high argument: an unresolved market question.

**The unmet ask, from a prospect:**
- Commenter `jordan_transitions` asked for coverage of how to explain AI PM
  experience in interviews, saying he struggles with it
  (`YOUTUBE_COMMENTS` row 5, 27 likes, on *From PM to AI PM: Real Talk*).
  Nothing in the data indicates this was answered. See
  [[feature-gaps-and-requests]].

## Interpretation
Three signals line up. Students buy confidence to make a transition. A hiring
manager publicly cannot find people who have made it. A prospect asks
specifically how to *narrate* the transition in an interview and gets no
recorded answer.

The gap between "I feel confident" and "I was hired" is where this business's
strongest possible testimonial lives, and we are not measuring it.

## Open Questions
- **Did any of the three transitioning students get placed?** No table records
  employment outcomes. This is the highest-value missing data in the wiki: a
  placement rate would answer [[pricing-objections]] more decisively than any
  marketing copy.
- Does the curriculum cover interview narrative and portfolio positioning at
  all? `YOUTUBE_COMMENTS` row 5 suggests a prospect thinks it might not.
- Is the transitioning segment (3 of 11) representative, or an artifact of who
  bothers to write private notes?
- Could the fintech hiring manager (`LINKEDIN_POSTS` row 5) be a hiring partner
  rather than only a demand signal?
