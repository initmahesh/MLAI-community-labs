# Job Strategy Document — Mahesh Yadav
*Generated July 10, 2026 · Target: VP/GM AI Product Leadership (with a founder track running in parallel) · Timeframe: 3-6 months*

## 1. Diagnosis

### Current Situation Analysis
Mahesh is currently employed full-time and searching from a position of strength, not urgency — which changes the calculus of this whole plan: he can be selective and doesn't need to take the first offer. His track record is genuinely rare: Product Management Leader for GCP AI agents (Jan 2024 – Apr 2025), Principal PM for Amazon Bedrock Agents (Sep 2022 – Jan 2024), Technical PM on Meta's PyTorch Distributed/AI Infrastructure team (Sep 2020 – Sep 2022), and nearly a decade at Microsoft spanning Azure ML/AI, IoT, and LTE/5G (2011–2020), including a run as a Senior Software Engineering Manager. Very few candidates in the market have shipped agentic AI products at CEO-keynote scale at three of the four hyperscalers.

The résumé also carries technical weight that most PM-track candidates can't match: 9 patents in power management, resource allocation, and computer vision; direct fluency in JAX, TensorFlow, PyTorch, LangGraph, Autogen, DeepSpeed, and ONNX; and coding ability in Python, C#, C++, and Java. That combination — product vision plus the ability to go toe-to-toe with a research or infra team — is the core asset this strategy is built around.

### Challenges and Opportunities
**Challenges**
- Location is the binding constraint on this search — Mahesh's whole career has been built inside a narrow set of hubs (Sunnyvale, Santa Clara, Menlo Park, Redmond), and VP/GM-scope AI roles outside those hubs are thin. This shrinks the addressable role pool more than seniority or comp does.
- The jump from Principal/Director-level IC and cross-functional leadership to formal VP/GM scope is a real level jump, not a title change — it needs to be argued for explicitly in every conversation, not assumed from the resume alone.
- Running a real search at 5-15 hrs/week while employed full-time means the clock (3-6 months) is tight relative to the depth of vetting a VP/GM search usually requires.

**Opportunities**
- The technical-depth-first positioning is a genuine differentiator: most VP/GM AI candidates are polished operators who talk about AI in the abstract; Mahesh can open a PyTorch distributed training internals conversation or a RAG/hallucination-reduction architecture discussion and hold his own. That's the wedge.
- The 501c3 non-profit (Cloud/AI access for non-profits) is real founder proof — it shows he can identify a gap, build something from zero with no corporate scaffolding, and sustain it. That's exactly the evidence a VP/GM search or a future venture needs.
- A "human + agent" org-design skill set is becoming a distinct, scarce leadership competency (see market analysis below) — Mahesh's actual shipped experience managing agent-based systems in production puts him ahead of that curve rather than reacting to it.

### Market and Competitive Analysis
The market data backs up both the opportunity and the urgency. US job postings requiring AI skills grew 144% year-over-year as of April 2026, and by May, postings with AI skills had more than doubled versus a year prior — demand is real and accelerating ([Gloat, AI Workforce Trends 2026](https://gloat.com/blog/ai-workforce-trends/)). At the same time, AI product managers are commanding a 20-22% premium over standard senior PMs at the same level, and companies that underprice the band lock out the senior tier of candidates and lengthen their own search by three to six weeks — a strong signal that Mahesh should not underprice himself in comp conversations ([KORE1, How to Hire an AI Product Manager: 2026 Guide](https://www.kore1.com/how-to-hire-ai-product-manager-2026/)).

On the leadership side specifically: only 22% of leaders are seen as capable of managing "human + agent" teams effectively, and roughly half of organizations are now formally structuring teams as human-agent units on the org chart ([Korn Ferry / Microsoft Work Trend Index 2026](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/worklab/work-trend-index/agents-human-agency-and-the-opportunity-for-every-organization)). That's a direct opening for someone who has actually run agent-based production systems, not just PM'd around them.

On the founder side, the agentic AI funding environment is hot but maturing: $510B poured into startups in H1 2026 with AI-driven deals accounting for nearly half of all VC, and first financings captured 40%+ of that capital — meaning strong new companies can still raise big rounds ([New Market Pitch, Agentic AI Market Funding Trends 2026](https://newmarketpitch.com/blogs/news/agentic-ai-funding-trends)). But investor focus has shifted from "what can agents do" to "can agents be monitored, governed, secured, and recovered when they fail" — which plays directly to Mahesh's Bedrock Guardrails and GCP agent-evaluation-framework experience, both of which are exactly this kind of governance work ([Gravity, AI Agent Funding Tracker Q3 2026](https://gravity.fast/blog/ai-agent-funding-tracker-q3-2026/)).

## 2. Guiding Policy

### Overall Approach
The strategy is technical-depth-first: lead every conversation — resume, outreach, and interviews — with the specific technical and architectural work (knowledge-graph RAG, agent evaluation frameworks, FSDP/3D parallelism, Guardrails) rather than generic "AI product leader" language. This is the wedge that separates Mahesh from the much larger pool of VP/GM candidates who can talk about AI strategy but can't defend an architecture decision. Given the location constraint, this positioning also needs to do double duty: it should be strong enough to justify remote or hub-flexible conversations that a generic PM resume wouldn't earn.

### Core Principles and Values
- Compensation must increase from current level — no lateral moves, even for a stronger title.
- The role must include real people-leadership scope (an org, not just influence-without-authority), consistent with the VP/GM target.
- The founder track (via the non-profit) runs in parallel, not as a fallback — it's a genuine second door, and decisions in the next 3-6 months should keep both doors open rather than force a premature choice.

## 3. Strategic Objectives

### Long-term Goals
In three years, Mahesh is Founder/CEO of his own AI venture — most plausibly one that grows out of or is validated by the non-profit's Cloud/AI-for-nonprofits work, or a new venture in the agentic AI governance/evaluation space where he already has deep, shipped expertise (Bedrock Guardrails, GCP agent evaluation standards).

### Short-term Objectives (SMART)
- Land a VP/GM-scope AI product leadership offer within 3-6 months that increases total compensation and includes direct people-leadership scope, prioritizing roles in or flexible toward his current location hub.
- Convert the non-profit from a side project into a demonstrable venture proof point within 6 months — a clear before/after narrative (impact metrics, structure, what he built solo) that can be told in 2 minutes to either an interview panel or a future investor.
- Build a short list of 15-20 target VP/GM roles and 10-15 warm-intro paths through his Google/AWS/Meta/Microsoft network within the first 6 weeks, so the search doesn't rely on cold applications.

- Rebuild the resume and LinkedIn headline around the technical-depth-first positioning, leading with the agent-evaluation, RAG/hallucination, and Guardrails work rather than generic product-leadership language.

Given the time squeeze risk flagged below, Mahesh should actively recruit 1-2 people from his network (former Bedrock, GCP, or Meta colleagues) as informal sounding boards for interview prep and positioning — not as a formal team, but to avoid the search running entirely on his own bandwidth.

## 6. Risk Management

### Potential Risks
- Time squeeze from the full-time job means the search stalls or loses momentum, especially in weeks where the day job spikes.
- Location constraint means the qualified role pool is genuinely small, so even a well-run search could come up short within 3-6 months.
- Interviewers or recruiters default to sizing him as "Senior/Principal PM" rather than VP/GM, given the resume shows individual title variance across companies rather than a clean upward trajectory to VP.
- Splitting attention between the job search and the non-profit/founder track could slow both down if not managed deliberately.

### Mitigation Plans
- Protect a fixed weekly block (not "whenever there's time") for search activity, and prioritize the highest-leverage 5-15 hrs — warm outreach and interview prep over passive job-board browsing.
- Treat location as a filter, not a wish: build the target list around roles that are genuinely open to his hub or explicitly remote-friendly, rather than applying broadly and hoping.
- Preempt the leveling issue directly — open conversations with a one-line frame ("I've been operating at VP/GM scope on AI agent product lines at Google, AWS, and Meta") rather than letting the resume's title variance speak for itself.
- Set an explicit decision checkpoint (Month 6 in the roadmap above) rather than letting the founder-vs-exec question stay open indefinitely — by then, both the search and the non-profit's proof-point work should have enough signal to decide.
